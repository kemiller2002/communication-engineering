# Mission

## Progress update — 2026-07-30

Desk research is complete and partial `RP-COMM-2026-0002` defines the
provisional measure hierarchy and scoring specification. The mission remains
active because reliability, sensitivity, burden, and accessibility feasibility
require candidate tasks and a named audience from RQ-COMM-001.

## Objective

Determine which outcome measures can validly, reliably, sensitively, and
ethically evaluate communication for informing and instructing, and specify the
minimum measure set for the first procedural pilot.

## Why this matters

An experiment cannot distinguish better communication from a better-looking
proxy unless its constructs and failure modes are explicit. This mission
unblocks both pilot selection and experimental design.

## Scope

### Included

Comprehension, task success, critical errors, assistance, time/effort,
retention/transfer, confidence calibration, satisfaction, accessibility, and
harm guardrails. Include objective and subjective measures, metric divergence,
reliability, construct validity, sensitivity, burden, and gaming risks.

### Excluded

Full measurement models for persuasion, trust, deliberation, narrative, or
long-term behavior change; participant experimentation beyond small instrument
feasibility checks; a universal communication-quality score.

## Existing context

RQ-COMM-003 in `research/frontier/PORTFOLIO-ROADMAP.md`;
EV-COMM-2026-0001 through 0005; RP-COMM-2026-0001; NIST and ISO usability
frames; preliminary purpose-to-outcome matrix.

## Initial hypotheses

1. No single measure validly represents overall communication effectiveness.
2. For procedural communication, unassisted correct completion is the best
   primary outcome when paired with critical-error, assistance, accessibility,
   and accuracy guardrails.
3. Satisfaction, readability, and time are useful diagnostic or secondary
   measures but unsafe primary optimization targets.
4. Delayed transfer is necessary before claiming learning rather than immediate
   instruction following.

## Required evidence

Primary measurement standards; validated instruments where applicable;
systematic reviews and controlled studies; counterexamples where subjective and
objective outcomes diverge; reliability/validity evidence; burden and
accessibility analysis.

## Constraints

Do not manufacture a composite score. Distinguish formative diagnostics from
summative outcomes. State whether each measure is criterion-, construct-, or
proxy-based. Preserve null and conflicting evidence.

## Execution instructions

1. Predefine constructs and inclusion criteria.
2. Search human factors, psychometrics, education, health communication, and
   technical communication.
3. Build an evidence-backed measure matrix with operational definitions.
4. For every measure record intended construct, known confounds, gaming risk,
   accessibility risk, collection burden, and decision rule.
5. Select primary, secondary, and guardrail measures for the proposed pilot.
6. Define a small instrument-feasibility test and stopping rule.

## Deliverables

REP, journal, evidence and hypothesis updates, accepted or revised measurement
matrix, pilot scoring specification, research debt, and portfolio review.

## Success criteria

The first pilot has a justified primary outcome, guardrails, scoring rules,
known validity limits, and evidence that the measures are feasible to collect.
A successor can implement the instrument without choosing constructs ad hoc.

## Stop conditions

Stop and narrow if the purpose classes require incompatible constructs or if
available evidence cannot support a pilot measure decision. Do not broaden into
a universal quality index.

## Handoff requirements

Provide exact operational definitions, scoring examples, missing-data handling,
participant burden, accessibility accommodations, analysis boundaries,
citations, and the next discriminating test.
