# Mission

## Objective

Determine how explanatory progression differs across one landmark primary work
from science, mathematics, law, philosophy, and engineering, and identify which
observed mechanisms are plausible cross-domain pattern candidates.

## Why this matters

The program mandate requires comparative matrices, a communication taxonomy,
and an engineering pattern catalog. A small, deliberately heterogeneous corpus
can test the comparison method and produce candidate patterns without claiming
that five works represent whole disciplines.

## Scope

### Included

- One primary work from each of five domains.
- Works available in a stable, citable edition and suitable for analysis in
  their original language or a declared translation.
- A fixed rubric covering audience, objective, prerequisite assumptions,
  information order, unit-to-unit progression, evidence or warrant,
  abstraction, examples, transitions, omission, pacing, memorability, failure
  modes, and transfer conditions.
- Within-work analysis, cross-domain comparison, negative cases, alternative
  explanations, and method-reliability assessment.
- Candidate pattern-library additions only when evidence and boundaries are
  explicit.

### Excluded

- Ranking authors or declaring a “greatest” communicator.
- Representing any culture, era, language, or discipline with one work.
- Causal claims about reader outcomes without experimental evidence.
- Exhaustive taxonomy, stylometry as a substitute for close analysis, and
  persuasion optimization.

## Initial hypotheses

1. Explanatory progression is conditioned more strongly by communicative
   purpose, audience prerequisites, and proof obligations than by prose style.
2. The domains share progression operations—orientation, decomposition,
   dependency ordering, warrant, example, and synthesis—but sequence and
   explicitness differ.
3. Domain conventions encode different acceptable omissions and burdens of
   proof, limiting direct transfer of otherwise successful patterns.
4. “Landmark” status is a survivorship- and institution-sensitive selection
   signal, not evidence of communication effectiveness.

## Required evidence

- Stable primary texts with edition and translation provenance.
- Independent historical or scholarly sources establishing original audience,
  purpose, context, and reception without treating reputation as outcome proof.
- At least one counterexample per proposed cross-domain pattern.
- Alternative explanations involving genre, translation, editorial history,
  institutional authority, and prerequisite knowledge.
- A second-rater or repeated-coding check on a defined sample before treating
  the rubric as reliable.

## Constraints

Freeze selection criteria and the coding rubric before full analysis. Separate
textual observation from interpretation and reception evidence. Record
translation dependence. Do not quote beyond applicable copyright limits.

## Execution instructions

1. Define “explanatory progression,” the unit of analysis, and observable codes.
2. Freeze corpus inclusion and exclusion criteria before choosing works.
3. Select candidate works; document selection bias and viable alternatives.
4. Pilot the rubric on matched excerpts; revise it once before freezing.
5. Code each work independently using the same rubric.
6. Construct the five-domain matrix and identify confirming, conflicting, and
   missing cases.
7. Test each candidate pattern against counterexamples and competing
   explanations.
8. Record only bounded pattern candidates with use conditions, failure modes,
   and confidence.
9. Produce the REP, journal, evidence/hypothesis updates, and program-mandated
   future research.

## Deliverables

- `RP-COMM-2026-0004`, with the complete REP structure.
- A versioned corpus manifest and frozen analytical rubric.
- A cross-domain comparison matrix.
- Provisional pattern candidates with use and non-use conditions.
- Evidence, hypothesis, journal, and theory-impact updates.
- At least five new questions, three competing hypotheses, experiments, missing
  disciplines, overlooked communicators, and quantitative-analysis options.

## Success criteria

Another analyst can reproduce corpus selection and coding; the comparison
distinguishes observation from interpretation; every cross-domain pattern has
confirming and counterevidence; transfer limits are stated; and the result
supports, narrows, or rejects at least one initial hypothesis.

## Stop conditions

Stop with a partial REP if stable texts or provenance cannot be obtained, the
rubric cannot reach usable agreement after one revision, or the selected works
prove incomparable under the declared unit. Narrow the corpus rather than
silently changing the question.

## Handoff requirements

Record exact editions, source locations, selection decisions, rejected works,
rubric version, coded units, disagreements, translation issues, search limits,
validation results, and the next discriminating study.
