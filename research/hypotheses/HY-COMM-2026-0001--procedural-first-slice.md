# Hypothesis

## Statement

A bounded procedural-documentation comparison is a better first pilot than an exhaustive style taxonomy or a persuasion study because it offers controlled inputs, observable user goals, low-risk synthetic tasks, and multiple outcome measures.

## Mechanism

Procedures map communication to visible actions. This makes failures, errors, effort, and successful completion observable while still exercising wording, sequencing, hierarchy, examples, and audience expertise.

## Predictions

- Two materially different instruction variants will produce detectable differences in at least one of task success, error count, time, or confidence calibration.
- Preference and task performance will sometimes diverge.
- Effects will vary with participant expertise and task complexity.

## Evidence that would support it

A preregistered pilot can recruit representative users, execute safely with synthetic material, and yield reliable outcome differences.

## Evidence that would contradict it

Recruitment is infeasible; realistic tasks require sensitive data; ceiling effects dominate; or outcomes cannot discriminate variants at feasible sample sizes.

## Tests performed

Comparative desk assessment against the mission criteria and established usability measurement frameworks. No participant experiment has run.

## Results

Provisionally supported for selecting the next research project, not yet validated as a product use case.

## Falsification attempts

Considered exhaustive taxonomy, generic readability scoring, persuasion, and high-stakes health/legal instructions. Taxonomy lacks a near-term behavioral criterion; readability is an inadequate outcome proxy; persuasion introduces ethical/confounding risks; high-stakes domains raise avoidable safety risks.

## Current assessment

Medium confidence. The decisive missing evidence is feasibility and stakeholder value for a specific procedural task.

## Next experiment

Compare two versions of a synthetic, multi-step software setup or data-transformation instruction with representative participants.
