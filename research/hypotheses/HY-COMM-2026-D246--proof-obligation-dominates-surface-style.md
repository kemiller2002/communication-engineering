# Hypothesis

## Statement

For bounded explanatory texts, communicative purpose, audience prerequisites,
and domain proof obligation predict the order and explicitness of progression
moves better than a writer's surface style label.

## Mechanism

Proof obligations determine what counts as a warrant and which dependencies
must be exposed; audience prerequisites and use mode determine orientation,
examples, navigation, and closure.

## Predictions

- Different writers in the same purpose/proof regime will share more
  progression operations than one writer across sharply different genres.
- Surface-style imitation without warrant architecture will not preserve task
  performance.

## Evidence that would support it

Larger preregistered corpus coding, within-writer genre comparisons, and reader
studies in which architecture predicts outcomes after controlling for lexical
and sentence-level variables.

## Evidence that would contradict it

Stable author-specific progression across genres, or surface transformations
that preserve or improve outcomes independent of proof architecture.

## Tests performed

Five-work comparative desk analysis using one frozen rubric and a 15-unit
repeated-coding stability sample.

## Results

All works manage orientation, prerequisites, warrants, and closure, but warrant
type and sequence align with task and proof regime. Support is provisional
because author and domain are confounded.

## Falsification attempts

Looked for author-like prose features that could explain the matrix. The RFC's
collective authorship and Brown's institutional voice weaken author essence as
a sufficient explanation; corpus size prevents decisive rejection.

## Current assessment

Provisionally supported at Medium (0.63 judgment).

## Next experiment

Code two genres by each of five authors and two authors per genre; fit a
multilevel model separating author, genre, proof obligation, and audience.
