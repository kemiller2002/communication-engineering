# Explanatory-progression coding rubric v1.0

**Frozen:** 2026-08-02 after one pilot revision.  
**Mission:** MS-COMM-2026-0004

## Coding protocol

1. Segment the bounded text where the dominant rhetorical function changes.
2. Assign one primary move and any secondary move from the codebook.
3. Record the explicit textual cue, dependency on prior material, warrant
   source, assumed prerequisite, omission, and plausible alternative reading.
4. Separate `O` observations from `I` interpretations and `H` untested outcome
   hypotheses.
5. Code once in source order, wait until the other works are coded, then recode
   a matched opening/middle/closing sample without consulting the first pass.
6. Resolve disagreements by narrowing claims; do not manufacture agreement.

## Codebook

| Code | Move | Observable test | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| OR | Orient | States situation, problem, purpose, or target | Background that has no local directional function |
| DF | Define | Fixes meaning, role, scope, or boundary | Mere synonym or example |
| PR | Prerequisite | Supplies or invokes a dependency needed later | Unmarked assumed knowledge |
| DC | Decompose | Splits a task, system, or claim into parts | A list with no dependency function |
| SQ | Sequence | Makes an order or dependency explicit | Chronology that does not guide inference/action |
| CL | Claim | Advances the target proposition | Summary of an already established proposition |
| WR | Warrant | Connects support to claim: proof, precedent, evidence, or norm | Support listed without an inferential bridge |
| EX | Example/analogy | Instantiates or maps an abstract relation | Evidence intended as direct warrant |
| OB | Objection/limit | Raises countercase, uncertainty, boundary, or exception | Generic qualification |
| RS | Resolution | Answers an objection or reconciles tension | Repetition of the initial claim |
| SY | Synthesize | Compresses prior moves into an integrated result | Local transition |
| NX | Navigate | Cross-reference, heading, numbering, or preview supports retrieval | Decorative formatting |
| AC | Action/closure | Specifies what follows, what is constructed, or what must be done | Descriptive consequence only |

## Required contextual fields

- intended audience and plausible resistance;
- audience starting state and target state;
- medium, length, and stakes;
- authority and proof obligation;
- unit and dependency boundaries;
- explicit versus implicit transitions;
- treatment of uncertainty and alternatives;
- accessibility and translation dependence;
- local strength, systemic cost, and transfer condition.

## Pilot revision and reliability check

The pilot initially combined `WR` and `EX`. Darwin's analogies, Brown's legal
precedent, Euclid's deductions, and RFC definitions showed that an example can
make a relation imaginable without warranting it under the domain's proof
rules. Version 1.0 therefore separates them and adds `NX` for specifications.

A repeated blind-to-first-pass recode covered each work's opening, one middle
unit, and closing (15 units). Primary-code agreement was 12/15 (80%). Three
disagreements were resolved as dual-coded moves: Darwin analogy (`EX+WR`),
Descartes rules (`DC+SQ`), and RFC terminology (`DF+NX`). This is an internal
stability check, not independent inter-rater reliability. A second analyst and
predeclared adjudication procedure remain required before using the rubric for
comparative measurement.

## What the rubric cannot establish

Move counts do not measure quality, comprehension, truth, or ethicality. The
rubric can expose architecture and generate outcome hypotheses; reader studies
are needed to determine effects.
