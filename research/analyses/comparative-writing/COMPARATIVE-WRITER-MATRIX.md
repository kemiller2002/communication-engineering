## Direct contrasts

### Auditability: Euclid versus Darwin

Euclid exposes a short dependency chain after premises are fixed. Darwin must
make a historical mechanism plausible across heterogeneous observations and
therefore alternates analogy, example, objection, and synthesis. Euclid is
preferable when the warrant system is closed and accepted; Darwin's architecture
is preferable when causal adequacy must be assembled. A hybrid can state
premises and dependencies explicitly while retaining counterexample testing,
but analogy must not be labeled proof [HY-COMM-2026-D246].

### Legitimate closure: Brown versus Descartes

Brown narrows an institutional question and speaks collectively; Descartes
builds identification through a personal journey. Use Brown's issue/holding
architecture when authority is real and action must close. Use Descartes's
problem-to-method arc when audiences need to revise their own reasoning.
Combining first-person intimacy with institutional authority can become
authority laundering unless roles are explicit.

### Retrieval: RFC 9110 versus linear argument

RFC 9110 supports non-linear expert lookup through definitions and links;
Darwin and Descartes reward continuous reading. A two-layer document—brief
linear orientation plus reference graph—is coherent for technical onboarding.
Dropping orientation and copying only specification density disadvantages
novices [HY-COMM-2026-CFF5].

## Cross-domain finding

All five works orient, manage prerequisites, provide domain-valid warrants, and
close, but the sequence and explicitness vary with the burden of proof and use
mode. This supports conditional fit and rejects “one best writer” selection.
