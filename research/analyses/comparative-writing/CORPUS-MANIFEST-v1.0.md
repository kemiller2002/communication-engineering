# Cross-domain explanatory-progression corpus manifest v1.0

**Frozen:** 2026-08-02  
**Mission:** MS-COMM-2026-0004  
**Unit:** one bounded argumentative or definitional sequence, coded as ordered
functional moves rather than as sentences alone.

## Definition

**Explanatory progression** is the observable order by which a text transforms
an audience's starting representation into a target representation by
orienting, defining, decomposing, connecting, warranting, exemplifying,
qualifying, and synthesizing claims. This definition concerns textual
architecture. It does not by itself establish comprehension or causality.

## Inclusion criteria (frozen before selection)

1. One work from science, mathematics, law, philosophy, and software
   engineering.
2. A stable, citable primary edition; translations must be declared.
3. A bounded sequence with an identifiable audience state, target state, and
   warrant or dependency structure.
4. Sufficient genre difference to stress-test the rubric, but a shared
   explanatory function sufficient for comparison.
5. Publicly inspectable text and independent context scholarship where
   available.

## Exclusion criteria

- Fame or reception as a proxy for reader outcomes.
- Whole-discipline representation from a single text.
- Sequences whose effect depends primarily on unavailable audio, visual, or
  performance evidence.
- Translations without edition provenance.
- Causal claims about comprehension without audience testing.

## Selected corpus

| Domain | Work and unit | Edition/provenance | Selection reason | Material limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Science | Charles Darwin, *On the Origin of Species* (1859), ch. IV, pp. 80–130 | First edition, Darwin Online, F373 | Mechanism explanation built through analogy, examples, objections, and synthesis | Chapter depends on earlier variation and struggle chapters |
| Mathematics | Euclid, *Elements*, Book I definitions/common notions and Proposition 1 | Joyce web edition based principally on Heath/Heiberg; translation is slightly modernized | Explicit dependency ordering and a canonical six-part proposition | Ancient authorship/editorial history and translation are uncertain |
| Law | U.S. Supreme Court, *Brown v. Board of Education*, 347 U.S. 483 (1954) | Official U.S. Reports scan via GovInfo | Compact institutional reasoning under precedent, legitimacy, and remedial constraints | Collective judicial voice; outcome and implementation span Brown I/II |
| Philosophy | René Descartes, *Discourse on the Method*, Part II | 1853 English translation on Wikisource; French original 1637 | Autobiographical problem framing leads to four procedural rules | Translation can alter rhythm and lexical claims |
| Engineering | Fielding, Nottingham, and Reschke, RFC 9110, §§1 and 3 | IETF Internet Standard, June 2022, current errata page | Layered terminology and normative cross-reference architecture | Consensus specification, not an individual writer or tutorial |

## Viable alternatives rejected

- Feynman lectures: strong teaching case, but lecture performance complicates
  text-only comparison.
- Hilbert's *Foundations of Geometry*: stronger modern axiomatics, but Euclid
  provides a more visible proposition microstructure.
- Holmes's *The Path of the Law*: individual legal voice, but less directly
  comparable to an opinion's institutional proof burden.
- Plato's dialogues: dialogic discovery is valuable but makes unit and speaker
  intent harder to code reliably in this first pilot.
- Hoare's 1969 axiomatic-programming paper: closer to mathematical proof than
  operational specification; RFC 9110 better tests layered retrieval.

## Selection bias and boundaries

The corpus is English-readable, heavily Western, male-authored or
institutionally voiced, and oriented toward elite written traditions. It is a
method pilot, not the diverse writer corpus required by the full program.
Non-Western, oral, Indigenous, feminine, vernacular, multimodal, and
accessibility-first traditions are missing and are high-priority research debt.
