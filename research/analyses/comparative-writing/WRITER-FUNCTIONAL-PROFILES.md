# Functional profiles: explanatory progression pilot

These profiles describe bounded works, not stable author essences. Confidence
is about textual function, not causal reader outcomes.

## Charles Darwin — *Origin*, chapter IV

### Context and purpose

Darwin addresses scientifically literate nineteenth-century readers facing a
mechanism that cannot be directly watched across geological time. Chapter IV
moves from prior premises about variation and struggle to natural selection,
divergence, and extinction.

### Functional techniques and structure

- `PR → EX/WR → CL → OB/RS → SY`: domestic selection supplies a familiar
  causal analogue; concrete cases accumulate; anticipated objections are
  addressed; the chapter periodically compresses the result.
- Rhetorical questions invite the reader to extend a known mechanism rather
  than receive only a declaration.
- Repeated conditional and modal language exposes inferential status, though
  cumulative examples can feel stronger than any single warrant.

### Fit and failure

Best for novel causal explanations where direct observation is incomplete and
the audience can carry a long chain. Poor for emergency instructions or compact
specifications. Transfer the progression from familiar mechanism to varied
cases to objections; do not transfer Victorian sentence texture or treat vivid
analogy as proof. **Confidence: medium-high** on architecture, low on audience
effect without testing [EV-COMM-2026-D6B2; EV-COMM-2026-6F00].

## Euclid — *Elements*, Book I and Proposition 1

### Context and purpose

The proposition addresses a learner or practitioner who accepts declared
definitions, postulates, and common notions. It transforms a requested
construction into a demonstrated result.

### Functional techniques and structure

- `CL(target) → AC(construction) → WR(dependency chain) → SY/AC(QED)`.
- Each inference exposes its dependency; the diagram carries information the
  prose alone does not fully duplicate.
- Repetition preserves referential precision and makes audit easier at the
  cost of conversational fluency.

### Fit and failure

Best for auditable derivations under shared primitives. Poor when premises are
contested, tacit knowledge is high, or motivation must precede formalism. Copy
the visible dependency chain, not the archaic wording. Translation/editorial
history and diagram dependence are non-transferable constraints. **Confidence:
high** on local structure, medium on historical audience [EV-COMM-2026-6A2D].

## U.S. Supreme Court — *Brown v. Board of Education*

### Context and purpose

The unanimous institutional opinion must decide consolidated constitutional
cases, justify departure from the practical effect of *Plessy*, and maintain
legal legitimacy amid intense resistance.

### Functional techniques and structure

- `OR(case/question) → OB(history inconclusive) → DF/PR(education's present
  role) → WR(effect and precedent) → CL/AC(holding)`.
- It narrows the question, acknowledges that original history is inconclusive,
  shifts to public education's present function and segregation's effect, then
  states a memorable categorical holding.
- Institutional understatement and unanimity carry authority; empirical
  footnoting is compact and later contested.

### Fit and failure

Best for high-stakes institutional decisions requiring narrow scope, explicit
issue framing, and legitimate closure. Poor as a full empirical synthesis or
implementation plan; Brown II handled remedy later. Transfer issue narrowing,
adverse-material acknowledgment, and holding clarity—not authority signals
detached from real authority. **Confidence: high** on textual order, medium on
the reasons for its institutional form [EV-COMM-2026-8434].

## René Descartes — *Discourse*, Part II

### Context and purpose

In a first-person vernacular discourse, Descartes narrates dissatisfaction with
received systems and presents four rules for directing his own inquiry.

### Functional techniques and structure

- `OR(narrative predicament) → EX(metaphors of cities/buildings) → OB(existing
  methods) → DC/SQ(four rules) → AC(personal program)`.
- Autobiography makes the need for method experiential before the rules become
  abstract.
- The four-rule compression aids recall, but the apparent sufficiency of a
  small method can overstate what practice requires; contemporary scholarship
  treats the rules as a condensed, practice-dependent summary.

### Fit and failure

Best for reflective method explanation and change of mental model. Poor for a
validated universal procedure or audiences who need direct steps first.
Transfer motivation-before-method and numbered decomposition; do not import
claims of certainty or a translation's surface voice. **Confidence: medium**
[EV-COMM-2026-5479; EV-COMM-2026-7D8D].

## Fielding, Nottingham, and Reschke — RFC 9110

### Context and purpose

The consensus Internet Standard must let implementers across HTTP versions
share semantics, find authoritative definitions, and distinguish normative
requirements from explanation.

### Functional techniques and structure

- `OR(purpose/scope) → DF(core concepts) → DC(layered abstractions) → NX
  (cross-references) → WR(normative requirements and examples)`.
- Stable terminology, section numbering, internal links, requirement keywords,
  and version-independent abstraction optimize retrieval and interoperability.
- Local passages often make sense only through a reference graph.

### Fit and failure

Best for reference, coordination, and error prevention among experts. Poor as a
novice tutorial or persuasive narrative. Transfer layered definitions,
normative keyword discipline, and examples adjacent to edge cases; add a
task-oriented entry path for novices. Do not mistake consensus status for
explanatory clarity. **Confidence: high** on structure, low on comparative
reader outcomes [EV-COMM-2026-3377].
