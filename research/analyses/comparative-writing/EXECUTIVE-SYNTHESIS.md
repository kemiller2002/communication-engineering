# Executive synthesis: choose functions, not famous voices

The first comparative pilot finds no defensible “best writer.” The useful unit
is a bounded communication function under explicit conditions.

Darwin helps when an audience must make an unseen mechanism plausible through
familiar analogies, varied cases, and answered objections. Euclid helps when a
derivation must be auditable from shared premises. *Brown* helps an authorized
institution narrow a question, acknowledge limits, connect governing warrant
to present effects, and close with a usable rule. Descartes helps motivate a
method by first making the problem experiential. RFC 9110 helps experts
coordinate through stable terminology, normative distinctions, and a navigable
reference graph.

The shared pattern is not sentence style. Each text manages prerequisites,
orders dependencies, supplies a genre-valid warrant, and closes according to
its use. The important differences follow proof burden, audience resistance,
authority, and use mode. Those variables should be declared before selecting
techniques.

Three guardrails matter most:

1. Analogy, fluency, authority, and memorability are not evidence.
2. Combining strong techniques is useful only when their audience roles and
   proof obligations are compatible.
3. High-stakes medical, safety, legal, organizational, and interpersonal
   communication requires domain protocols, accessibility, uncertainty, and
   audience welfare to constrain any writer-derived model.

The result supports `TH-COMM-2026-0001` at Medium confidence but remains a desk
study. The next discriminating step is a preregistered comparison of two
architectures on one explanation task, measuring comprehension, transfer,
calibrated confidence, and subgroup accessibility—not liking or readability
alone.
