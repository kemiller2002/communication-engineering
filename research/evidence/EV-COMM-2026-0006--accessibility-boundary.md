# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

W3C cognitive-accessibility guidance recommends understandable words, short sentences and blocks, unambiguous content, visual separation, summaries, and separated instructions for users with cognitive and learning disabilities.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports treating accessibility as a boundary condition in communication strategy selection, not an optional aesthetic preference.

## Source provenance

Official W3C Working Group Note produced by accessibility task-force participants. It is explicitly supplemental guidance, not a normative WCAG success criterion.

## Relevant excerpt or data

The guidance links specific patterns to barriers experienced by users with language, memory, attention, and learning impairments.

## Interpretation

Optimization for aggregate task performance must not erase accessibility constraints or exclude participants with disabilities.

## Limitations

The page is expert consensus guidance and user-needs synthesis, not a controlled estimate of each pattern's causal effect. W3C states that some cognitive needs are not covered by existing normative standards.

## Counterevidence

Technical precision may require specialized vocabulary; clarity should be evaluated for the specified audience rather than reduced to short words alone.

## Reproduction or verification notes

Inspect the status note and linked design patterns on the W3C page.
