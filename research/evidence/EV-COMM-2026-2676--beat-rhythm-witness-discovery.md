# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

Ginsberg's long anaphoric catalogue builds collective witness; Kerouac's prose
manifesto prioritizes associative drafting and rhythmic momentum; Ferlinghetti's
street-level observer uses spacing, humor, and civic collage. These are related
historically but functionally distinct.

## Claim and interpretation

Contradicts a single “Beat style.” Supports three bounded patterns: anaphoric
witness, generative spontaneity, and mobile civic observation.

## Provenance and limits

Primary works and Poetry Foundation contextual sources were inspected. Works
remain copyrighted; no extended text is reproduced. Performance, publication
history, obscenity litigation, collaboration, gender, race, and appropriation
require deeper specialist study.

## Counterevidence

Ferlinghetti rejected the fixed Beat label; “spontaneity” can underdescribe
revision and craft; Ginsberg's catalogue is highly structured.
