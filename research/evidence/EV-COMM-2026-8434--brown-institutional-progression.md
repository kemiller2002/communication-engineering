# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

The unanimous opinion identifies the consolidated constitutional question,
finds Fourteenth Amendment history inconclusive for public education, examines
education's contemporary function and segregation's effect, then states its
categorical holding while deferring remedy.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports the bounded claim that high-stakes institutional reasoning can combine
adverse-material acknowledgment, narrow framing, effect-based application, and
clear closure.

## Source provenance

Official United States Reports text, volume 347, pages 483–496, accessed through
the U.S. Government Publishing Office.

## Relevant excerpt or data

The opinion moves from cases and question to historical limits, public
education's role, inequality produced by separation, and the conclusion that
separate educational facilities are inherently unequal.

## Interpretation

The order manages both legal warrant and legitimacy. Unanimity and institutional
voice are contextual authority resources, not transferable prose tricks.

## Limitations

The opinion is a collective institutional product. Social-science footnote 11,
original-understanding analysis, implementation, and causal impact are debated;
Brown II contains the remedy.

## Counterevidence

Its compact empirical treatment is not a model for comprehensive evidence
synthesis, and its authority cannot be generalized to unauthoritative writers.

## Reproduction or verification notes

Read 347 U.S. 483–496 in sequence and separately track issue, historical
analysis, present function, effect, holding, and remedial deferral.
