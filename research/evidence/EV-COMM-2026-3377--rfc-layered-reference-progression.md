# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

RFC 9110 begins with protocol purpose and scope, establishes terminology and
core concepts, decomposes the protocol into layered abstractions, and relies on
numbered cross-references, normative keywords, examples, and exceptions.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports the claim that a specification can optimize expert retrieval and
interpretation agreement through a navigable reference graph rather than a
single linear teaching sequence.

## Source provenance

Official IETF Datatracker presentation of Internet Standard RFC 9110 with
status, authors, formats, references, and errata links.

## Relevant excerpt or data

Sections 1 and 3 state purpose and core semantics, define resources and roles,
and point forward to methods, status codes, content, and related standards.

## Interpretation

Navigation is part of the explanatory architecture. Requirement strength and
examples manage implementation risk, but the same structure imposes novice
orientation cost.

## Limitations

The document is a consensus product, not an individual writer profile. No user
study was reviewed comparing novice or expert comprehension of this RFC.

## Counterevidence

RFC 9110 includes accessible explanatory passages; specification architecture
is not necessarily opaque. Its current modularization also reflects document
maintenance and versioning needs, not cognition alone.

## Reproduction or verification notes

Inspect §§1, 3, 6, and 9; trace references required to understand one request
method and distinguish semantic explanation from normative requirement.
