# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

NIST operationalizes effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction using measures including unassisted task success, time on task, errors, ease ratings, and observed issues.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports the feasibility of a small controlled comparison of two procedural communication variants.

## Source provenance

Official NIST interagency report. It applies human-factors methods in electronic health records and cites established usability measurement literature.

## Relevant excerpt or data

For summative tests, the report recommends quantitative participant performance measures such as task success, time, and errors; formative work can add open-ended responses and issue counts.

## Interpretation

The first vertical slice can produce a multi-metric outcome rather than collapsing quality into readability or preference.

## Limitations

The domain is interactive health software, not standalone writing. Time can be misleading when careful deliberation is desirable.

## Counterevidence

No single metric indicates overall communication quality; measures must be selected against the intended outcome.

## Reproduction or verification notes

See NISTIR 7741, usability-testing measures section, especially page 50.
