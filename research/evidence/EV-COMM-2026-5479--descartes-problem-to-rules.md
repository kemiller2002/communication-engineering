# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

Part II narrates dissatisfaction with inherited systems, uses building/city
analogies and critiques prior methods, then compresses an alternative into four
ordered rules and a personal program.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports the textual claim that an abstract method can be introduced through a
felt problem and a small memorable decomposition.

## Source provenance

Proofread Wikisource transcription of an 1853 public-domain English
translation; original French publication was 1637.

## Relevant excerpt or data

The text explicitly describes proceeding cautiously, criticizes logic,
geometrical analysis, and algebra, and says four precepts can replace a large
number of rules.

## Interpretation

Autobiography motivates the abstraction, while numbering and sequence compress
the method. The personal narrative is not evidence of universal efficacy.

## Limitations

Translation affects diction, syntax, and rhythm. The Discourse accompanied
scientific essays; Part II alone does not demonstrate the method in practice.

## Counterevidence

Current scholarship notes that the four rules are a condensed summary and that
practice cannot be fully codified [EV-COMM-2026-7D8D].

## Reproduction or verification notes

Compare the 1853 Part II against the French Adam and Tannery edition before
making word-level or rhythm claims.
