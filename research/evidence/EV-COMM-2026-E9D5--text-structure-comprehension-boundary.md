# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

A meta-analysis identified 19 eligible studies and 48 studywise effect sizes
on interventions teaching expository text structures, reporting that such
instruction can improve comprehension while outcomes vary with intervention
features.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports only the general mechanism boundary that recognizing or signaling
structure can aid comprehension in studied educational contexts. It does not
validate the pilot's writer-to-purpose recommendations.

## Source provenance

Peer-reviewed *Reading Research Quarterly* meta-analysis. Publisher abstract
and study description were inspected; effect sizes and risk-of-bias details
were not extracted in this cycle.

## Relevant excerpt or data

The abstract reports 21 identified studies, 19 included, and 48 studywise
effect sizes, with analysis of structure types and instructional features.

## Interpretation

Text architecture is plausibly consequential, but reader knowledge and
instruction interact with the structure. This favors conditional rather than
universal recommendations.

## Limitations

The interventions teach text structures; they do not experimentally compare
Darwin, Euclid, legal opinions, philosophy, and RFCs. Full quantitative results
were not reviewed, so no effect magnitude is claimed.

## Counterevidence

Structure cues can add load, and transfer across age, language, expertise, and
genre remains uncertain.

## Reproduction or verification notes

Retrieve the full paper and extraction tables before using an effect estimate.
