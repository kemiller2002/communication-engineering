# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

In scenario-based testing with expert and novice nurses, subjective
task-completion survey ratings had weak or marginal correlations with observed
performance measures including task success and time.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports treating satisfaction, perceived ease, and confidence as diagnostic
secondary measures rather than proxies for correct completion.

## Source provenance

Peer-reviewed empirical pilot study available through PubMed Central. The
reported correlation analysis covered 108 expert-group and 63 novice-group
task observations, not independent participants.

## Relevant excerpt or data

The paper’s Table 3 reports correlations between task-completion survey ratings
and mouse activity, time on task, and percent task success; the authors
characterize the overall pattern as weak or marginal.

## Interpretation

Subjective experience remains useful for explaining adoption and friction, but
agreement with objective performance must be measured rather than assumed.

## Limitations

Clinical software, repeated task observations, small pilot samples, and
interface interaction may not generalize to standalone procedural documents.
Correlation does not establish causal direction.

## Counterevidence

Other studies find moderate associations between subjective and behavioral
measures. Divergence is possible, not inevitable; both channels are needed.
