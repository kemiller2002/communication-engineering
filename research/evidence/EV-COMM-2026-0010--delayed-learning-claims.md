# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

The IES practice guide synthesizes experimental learning research showing
forgetting over time, benefits of spaced retrieval, and improved accuracy from
delayed judgments of learning. It separates immediate performance from durable
knowledge and transfer.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports requiring a delayed no-instruction repeat or novel transfer task
before claiming that communication taught a reusable skill. It does not make
delay mandatory for the narrower claim that a document enabled immediate task
completion.

## Source provenance

U.S. Department of Education evidence practice guide with explicit evidence
ratings and technical source notes. It is a synthesis, not a single experiment.

## Relevant excerpt or data

The guide rates spacing evidence Moderate, documents substantial initial
forgetting, and describes delayed judgments/tests as more informative than
immediate feelings of mastery.

## Interpretation

The portfolio must distinguish “supports execution while present” from
“teaches a skill retained or transferred later.” The first pilot can test the
former; a learning claim adds delayed assessment and participant burden.

## Limitations

Most cited work concerns academic learning, often laboratory content. It does
not establish the best delay interval or transfer task for technical
procedures.

## Counterevidence

For just-in-time documentation, durable memory may be irrelevant or even
undesirable; adding delayed testing would then measure the wrong construct.
