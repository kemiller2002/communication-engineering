# Evidence Record

## Summary and claim

Primary works and movement sources show formal sonnets, blues/jazz vernacular,
folklore, montage, drama, manifesto, neighborhood lyric, feminist/queer address,
and internationalist solidarity. No single “Black style” or audience contract
fits Hughes, Hurston, McKay, Toomer, Johnson, Baraka, Sanchez, Giovanni, Brooks,
Jordan, and Lorde.

## Provenance and limitations

Poetry Foundation movement collections and author records, plus public primary
works where available. Movement boundaries and the “adjacent” status of Brooks,
Jordan, and Lorde are interpretive; the guide states rather than erases that.

## Counterevidence

Shared institutions, racial domination, music, community publishing, and
political struggle create real relations across the works despite difference.
