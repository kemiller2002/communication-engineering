# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

Chapter IV progresses from artificial selection as a familiar causal model to
natural cases, conditional inferences, divergence, extinction, objections, and
periodic synthesis. The first-edition source preserves page and chapter order.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports the textual claim that a mechanism with incomplete direct observation
can be developed through a familiar causal bridge plus heterogeneous cases and
objection handling. It does not prove that this structure improves learning.

## Source provenance

Darwin Online digital transcription of the 1859 John Murray first edition,
catalogue F373, inspected in Chapter IV.

## Relevant excerpt or data

The chapter explicitly asks what nature can effect after establishing what
human selection can effect, follows with concrete organisms and conditional
reasoning, and later synthesizes consequences for extinction and divergence.

## Interpretation

The dominant progression is analogical-causal rather than a closed deductive
proof. Examples perform both explanatory and evidentiary roles, which must be
distinguished in coding.

## Limitations

Chapter IV depends on earlier chapters. Historical readership and reception
are not measured. Modern biology validates many conclusions but does not by
itself validate the original rhetoric.

## Counterevidence

The chapter also contains explicit logical transitions and is not reducible to
analogy. Scholarly interpretations disagree about the structure of the whole
book [EV-COMM-2026-6F00].

## Reproduction or verification notes

Inspect Chapter IV around pp. 80–130 and compare its opening causal analogy,
worked examples, divergence section, extinction synthesis, and recap.
