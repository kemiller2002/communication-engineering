# Evidence Record

## Summary and claim

Anzaldúa's code-switching, Cha's multilingual archive and silence, Lee's
intimate diasporic memory, Baldwin's implicated moral address, Jordan/Lorde's
intersectional solidarity, and Vuong's fragment and epistolary distance assign
readers unequal access intentionally. Fluency cannot be the only evaluation
criterion.

## Provenance and limitations

Author records, hosted works, and canonical bibliographies were reviewed.
“Immigrant” and “queer” are overlapping contexts, not genres; Baldwin source
coverage in this cycle was less systematic than the poetry records.

## Counterevidence

Many works also pursue direct accessibility and broad solidarity. Partial
access is a function in some texts, not a rule for all diasporic or queer work.
