# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

In a blinded randomized international online trial with 488 adults, plain-language recommendations improved comprehension substantially for one health recommendation but not significantly for another; accessibility and satisfaction improved for both.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports the claim that plain language can improve outcomes but is not uniformly sufficient, and that comprehension and preference are distinct.

## Source provenance

Peer-reviewed controlled superiority trial with allocation concealment, blinded assessment framing, registered outcome measures, and a linked qualitative study. PubMed record and abstract inspected.

## Relevant excerpt or data

WHO item comprehension: mean difference 19.8 percentage points (95% CI 14.7–24.9). CDC item: 3.9 points (95% CI -0.7–8.3). Accessibility and satisfaction favored the plain-language versions.

## Interpretation

The baseline text and information structure moderate intervention effects. A test must compare outcomes per task and must not infer comprehension from satisfaction.

## Limitations

Online English-proficient sample was demographically uneven; only two COVID-19 vaccine recommendations were compared.

## Counterevidence

The large WHO-item effect supports plain language as a promising component when the baseline is difficult.
